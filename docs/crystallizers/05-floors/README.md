## 2. Goal

A macroform landmark is a large zone of playable terrain (a city, a castle, a mountain at the scale of a 2 km map), not a solid block and not relief without interiors. The exterior is the shell, the view from outside; the floors are the playable interior, each one a full 2.5D map at real scale. The 40x40 demo with two flat floors is only a simplification of that.
Goal: compose N stacked heightmaps of ONE landmark (an exterior shell and its interior floors) and present them three ways, reusing Box / A / B / C unchanged. The question it answers: can a multi-floor macroform be read from its 2.5D maps (exterior, floors, the link between them)?
Not the SDF exterior (deferred, it may become a later crystallizer): no volume representation, no WebGL.

## 3. Decisions

- D1 The mountain is hollow along a central vertical SHAFT, open at the top as a CRATER (a sheer pit, r 2.5 around (20.5, 20.5), dug from the plateau at ~7.4 down to floor 1). The shaft replaces the serpent tube as the link between floors: an intentional deviation from GDD 4.1 (the serpent only gives the exterior, the floor-height reference and the head anchor). No rooms layer inside the floors.
- D2 Floors are INTERIOR LEVELS inside a shell, not sections of the exterior solid and not zones (a zone is a slice of one map). The exterior is the shell: the view from outside (overworld). Each floor is the playable interior, a full 2.5D map in its own right. In the 40x40 demo, floor footprints are derived with a rule (exterior >= z + 1.0 plus a shell of 1.5 tiles) only to get plausible interior footprints under the shell; the rule is the demo's way to fake interior data, not a model of how interiors are built. Two floors at z = 3.6 and 5.2, no third floor (the footprint at z ~ 6 has ~110 tiles; not requested).
- D3 The derived package is `data/snake_mountain_floors.json` (`tools/derive_snake_floors.py`, numpy only, reproduces the JSON bit for bit, reads only `data/snake_mountain_surface.json`). `snake_surface` is untouched (default source and regression baseline). The pack declares a viewer block `{ terraces: 5, minPlateau: 5 }` (choosing the source sets both; 5 is the band layout the floor heights were chosen for; the declared link does not depend on them).
- D4 GLOBAL height frame: every layer carries the package's `elevFrame` (the surface range 1.256 .. 9.115) so terrace heights agree between floors. `quantize` in `src/shape.js` honours it ONLY when present (also where the map has void); without it nothing changes (`elevRange` behaviour untouched; `tools/test_floors.js` compares the terraces with origin/main).
- D5 The head `H` is the high end of the spiral, (22, 16), in THIS pack only; the source pack keeps (29, 26) (the inverted end from the spreadsheet). `W`, `P`, `E` stay where they are (placed for the old head); the semantic-landmark stage will resolve it. The head is a semantic anchor, not built here.
- D6 The link between floors is a DECLARED LINK (section 5): the pack declares it, a forced site carves its ramp; it is not detected. Reason: GDD 4.1 step 5 says floor connections are RESERVED (declared by the cave graph), the gate criterion is for natural terrain transitions. A straight radial ramp, not an arc or a spiral (later improvements).
- D7 Number reference: an earlier estimate of the centre height (5.1) came from the cave-carved composite, not from the repository. The pure surface has 7.41 at (20.5, 20.5), a plateau of 7.0-7.5 up to r ~ 4 and the crest (9.1) at r 5-6; there is no basin, so the crater is dug. Section areas (z + 1, shell 1.5, shaft r 2.5): z = 3: 289, z = 4: 223, z = 5: 161 tiles.

## 4. Data (derivation rules)

The demo's floors are DERIVED data (`tools/derive_snake_floors.py`), a stand-in for interiors that a real landmark would author. The rules only give plausible interior levels inside the shell:
- Exterior (the shell) = the surface with the shaft pit (tiles whose centre is within 2.5 of the axis lowered to z1 = 3.6).
- Interior level k = a footprint under the shell: the tiles with exterior >= z_k + 0.15 + 1.0 (a roof margin counted from the highest floor tile), kept 1.5 tiles away from the shell's outline; the shaft is void in floor 2, floor 1 keeps the disc as the flat pit bottom; deterministic bumps <= 0.15 (zero in the link box). Floor 1: 264 tiles, floor 2: 136; floor 2 lies inside floor 1.
- Keys of the pack (optional, old packs unaffected; `docs/pack-format.md`): `floors`, `shaft`, `links`, `elevFrame`; helpers `E.hasFloors`, `E.validateFloors`, `E.floorPack(pack, k)` (k = 0 the exterior), `E.floorWarnings(pack, S)` in `src/fields.js`, `E.linkProtect` in `src/shape.js`.

## 5. The link between floors (declared links, plan B)

The pack declares the link (`links[0]`): the pair `site` a = (13, 20) (a ring tile of floor 1, z1) and b = (14, 20) (the first landing tile, z2), its own `width` 3 and `depth` 2, the `landing` rectangle and the `footprint` rectangle. In `src/shape.js` (opt-in: only a pack with `links`, carried by the floor the link starts from, `E.floorPack`):
- `addDeclaredLinks`: a gate record and a FORCED site (`exact`, `declared`) at the pair, bypassing the gate detection (precedent: `site.exact` / `S.makeSite` of the rooms layer); the declared sites go FIRST in `S.passes`, so a natural stair never takes their tiles; natural gate sites that touch the link's tiles are dropped;
- `carveStairs` plans a declared site with ITS OWN width x depth (`planRamp` overrides), always as a ramp, and builds it even when stairW is 0: declared links are structure, the stair sliders govern natural stairs only;
- `linkProtect` (`E.linkProtect`): the landing, the footprint and the ring foot tiles are excluded from the pre-smoothing blur and from the plateau cleanup (`minPlateau`, `minSub`), so presets like Balanced types (minPlateau 120) cannot merge the landing into the ring.
Geometry (`tools/derive_snake_floors.py`): the ring of floor 1 at z1 (x 11..13), a FLAT landing at z2 = 5.2 (x 14..17, plus x 18 where r >= 2.5; lanes y 18..22: 22 tiles) next to it (a cliff of 1.6), the ramp CUT into the landing (footprint x 14..15 x y 19..21: 3 x 2 = the viewer's default stairW and rampDepth; end tile x 16). Floor 2 has the void OPENING over exactly the footprint (6 tiles) and keeps the ARRIVAL ledge (x 16..17, 2 tiles) between the opening and the shaft; floor 2's ring stays connected (the opening is a hole in it) and so does floor 1's around the landing (both tested). Every arrival tile of floor 1 is a walkable floor-2 tile at exactly z2.

### The knife-edge attempt (dropped)
Step B first tried to let the gate detection find the link: a sloped strip rising to the landing. Measured on the viewer pipeline: two flat plateaus give no gate (the criterion compares band-normalised slopes: 0.245 vs 0.255 for z1 and z2 at 5 terraces, difference -0.01 < `gateThr` 0.05); strips narrower than 5 lose the gate to the pre-smoothing (the gate group falls under `gateMin` 3); with 3 slope tiles (the spike's geometry) the depth-2 ramp was dropped on this package; one slope tile at z = 4.4 worked, but only because it sat exactly on the band edge of 5 terraces (4.39995), a float tie that breaks with the pre-smoothing, `gateThr`, `gateMin` or the bumps (values 3.8 .. 4.2 gave 0 of 20 combinations). It also depended on the number of terraces (3 and 5 only) and on `minPlateau`. It was dropped for declared links.

## 5b. The Floors view (step C, `src/floors.js`, `E.renderLayers` in `src/render.js`, controls in `src/ui.js`)

Selector "Floors view" (only for a floors package) + chips Exterior / Floor 1 / Floor 2:
- SINGLE FLOOR: the existing pipeline on `E.floorPack(raw, k)` (Box / A / B / C, compare mode as always). The package-wide camera frame (`S.fitH`) and thin floor slabs (`S.baseH` a little under the lowest level, the precedent of the Pocket) are the same in every layer, so switching layers never rescales.
- EXPLODED STACK: floor 1, floor 2 and the exterior drawn bottom to top by the normal pipeline of the technique, each raised by a height offset (`zShift`, a rigid screen translation for the orthographic cameras), the exterior on top as a GHOST lid (drawn on a transparent canvas and composited once at 0.42, so overlapping polygons do not accumulate). The gap is the floor-1 extent along the view depth times tan(pitch), clamped 6-30.
- CUTAWAY: a PLANE perpendicular to the view through the shaft axis (`cam.ry(x, y) = cam.ry(axis)`, the camera depth coordinate, so it follows the preset yaw AND the drag variation) uncovers part of a floor: the BACK half of the exterior shell is drawn first, then the floors cropped to the FRONT half at their true heights. The interior volume between layers is not modelled, and the cut face is a section of the heightfield (a staircase, a curve or a smoothed edge depending on the technique), not rock; the floors therefore read as slabs in front of the cut. In A / B / C the floor walls hang to the lowest grid level (a deep skin), so the floors look like thick blocks (Box keeps a thin slab). Painter order: measured against a z-buffer in 5c.
- Orthographic cameras only (Cam 1-5 and 7); with Cam 6 the stack and the cutaway say so and show the selected floor. Stake and objectives are placed in the single-floor views only (the stack and the cutaway say so).
- The info line names the view and the layer, the declared link ("declared ramp 3 x 2 carved (cut)") and warns when a declared link was not built.
- A floors package shares ONE centre terrace (`elevFrame` implies the 'middle' centre in `quantize`), so the layer heights agree whatever the spread.
Measured (`node tools/test_floors.js` 32 checks, `tools/ui/test_floors_ui.js` 19 checks): compare mode, all four panels, cold: 0.42 s (stack) and 0.45 s (cutaway) to the last panel; the cut plane tiles are all behind / in front (800 exterior tiles, 185 floor tiles at the first yaw).
HOW IT READS (screenshots `tools/verify.sh shots --extra floors`, Cam 1 and Cam 3, Box / A / B / C; 45-55 px per tile at game scale):
- the CRATER (sheer pit, A at 45 px): a clear dark hole in the plateau with the tan floor 1 at the bottom; at the whole-map fit it is only a few pixels and the markers C and S overlap. A stepped inner wall (a later option) would give it depth.
- the LINK on floor 1: a raised tan landing (5 wide, a 1.6 cliff to the ring) with the cream ramp (3 wide, cross lines) cut into it: locatable at once.
- the ARRIVAL LEDGE on floor 2: two tiles of floor between the opening and the shaft; it reads as a narrow neck between two holes, a bridge. The opening itself reads as a second pit: nothing says "the stairs go down here" (the ramp is in floor 1's map); a marker, a rail or a stair mark is the obvious later addition.
- the CUT FACE (game scale): Box a tile-exact staircase; A a wavy edge with the sub-level banding of the wall; B a smooth edge; C a slightly eroded edge (its corner rule displaces the cut like any other contour). In all of them the floors read as slabs in front of the cut, floating (no solid material between the floors, the exterior is a heightfield): a limit of the representation.
- the STACK: the floors are separate and readable, the ghost lid shows where the mountain sits; the marker labels of the lid and of the floors overlap.

### Experiment discarded: ghost exterior in the Cutaway

Tried in scratch (nothing applied): the back half of the exterior drawn as the translucent ghost of the Stack lid (opacity 0.42), (a) ghost first (same order as now) and (b) ghost last (over the floors), in Box / A / C, Cam 1 and Cam 3, fitted and at 50 px per tile. Discarded: (a) leaves the shell BEHIND the floors as a pale backdrop, so the floors still read as slabs in front of the cut, not as inside a shell; (b) breaks the painter order (the exterior behind the plane would be drawn over the floors in front of it); and the back half of the floors is cropped, so nothing of the interior behind the plane is shown. A full ghost exterior over whole floors is just the Stack lid, no longer a cut. Cost if ever wanted: `E.renderLayers` already supports `ghost` (mark the first layer of the cut list with `ghost: GHOST` in `layers()` of `src/floors.js`; one more line to reorder for (b)); `tools/ui/test_cut_ui.js` measures coverage with alpha >= 128 and the ghost composites at about 107, so that layer would have to be rendered without the ghost for the mask.

## 6. Known limits

- MEASURED (`node tools/test_floors.js`): the declared link is carved (3 columns x depth 2) and walkable from the foot to the arrival for terraces 2-12 x pre 0-3 (44 combinations), also for stairW 0-5 x rampDepth 1-4 and for minPlateau 120 / minSub 100: a jump of 1, 2 or 3 terraces carves the same (`planRamp` is generic in the terraces of its two tiles). Floors at 3.6 / 5.2; terrace jump of the pair: 1 at 2-5 terraces, 2 at 6, 1 at 7, 2 at 8-9, 3 at 10, 2 at 11, 3 at 12.
- The floors' OWN rings are ordinary terrain: at 10 terraces z1 = 3.6 lies on a band edge (3.614), the bumps and the smoothing split floor 1's ring into terraces and, with `pre` 2-3, the ring tile next to the ramp's foot is cut from it by a cliff ("ring cut" in the printed table). The link itself still works. Bands at 5 terraces: 1.26 / 2.83 / 4.40 / 5.97 / 7.54 / 9.12; at 7: 1.26 / 2.38 / 3.50 / 4.62 / 5.75 / 6.87 / 7.99 / 9.12.
- The Floors view warns only when a declared link was not built in the lower floor (`E.floorWarnings(pack, S)`: the pair in void, the ramp did not fit): no slider setting is a reason to warn any more.
- The cutaway is orthographic only, plane-based and recomputed on release; its painter order is measured (5c: 0 % Box, 1.4-3.4 % A / B / C); in the cutaway the floors read as slabs in front of the cut (the volume between layers is not modelled); stack and cutaway build three shapes per layer set (cost measured above).
- The marker labels C and S overlap at the full view (and D / A in Cam 3, and most labels in the stack of Cam 4 and Cam 7).
- The ring tiles next to the landing read its raw 5.2 in the pre-smoothing blur and can change terrace (cosmetic; a natural stair may appear at that cliff, never on the link's tiles).
- With the opening sized exactly to the declared footprint, a link whose `width` / `depth` the pack changes needs the derivation to be re-run (the opening and the data stay in step).
- The link is a straight radial ramp, not an arc or a spiral.
- Not verified: touch input, non-Chromium browsers, GPU timings.
